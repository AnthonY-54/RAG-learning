The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is the national space agency of India. 
It is headquartered in Bengaluru, Karnataka, and operates under the Department of Space, 
which is directly overseen by the Prime Minister of India.

ISRO was founded on 15 August 1969 by Vikram Sarabhai, who is widely regarded as the 
father of the Indian space programme. Sarabhai recognized early that space technology 
could play a transformative role in national development. The agency started modestly, 
with its first rocket launch site established at Thumba, near Thiruvananthapuram, chosen 
because of its proximity to the Earth's magnetic equator.

ISRO's first satellite, Aryabhata, was launched on 19 April 1975, making India one of 
the early developing nations to enter the space age. The satellite was named after the 
ancient Indian mathematician and astronomer. The launch was carried out from a Soviet 
Cosmodrome, as India did not yet have its own launch vehicles.

A major milestone came with the development of the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV). 
The PSLV had its first successful flight in 1994 and has since become one of the world's 
most reliable launch vehicles. It has launched satellites for dozens of countries and is 
known for its versatility and high success rate.

In 2008, ISRO launched Chandrayaan-1, India's first lunar probe. The mission made a 
significant scientific discovery: it confirmed the presence of water molecules on the Moon's 
surface, a finding that reshaped global understanding of lunar resources. This was achieved 
using the Moon Mineralogy Mapper instrument, provided by NASA.

India's Mars Orbiter Mission, popularly called Mangalyaan, was launched in November 2013 
and successfully entered Martian orbit in September 2014. India became the first country 
to reach Mars on its first attempt and the first Asian nation to do so. The mission was 
completed at a remarkably low cost of approximately 74 million US dollars, far less than 
comparable missions by other agencies.

Chandrayaan-3 was launched on 14 July 2023. On 23 August 2023, the Vikram lander 
successfully touched down near the Moon's south pole, making India the fourth country 
to achieve a soft landing on the Moon and the first to do so at the lunar south pole. 
The Pragyan rover deployed from Vikram conducted surface experiments and confirmed the 
presence of sulphur and other elements near the lunar south pole.

ISRO's Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) was developed to launch heavier 
communication satellites into geostationary orbits. The GSLV Mark III, also called LVM3, 
is the agency's heaviest rocket and was used to launch the Chandrayaan-3 mission.

ISRO also operates a constellation of remote sensing satellites under the IRS (Indian 
Remote Sensing) series and communication satellites under the INSAT and GSAT series. 
These serve critical national needs including weather forecasting, disaster management, 
telecommunications, and agricultural monitoring.

India's first human spaceflight programme, Gaganyaan, is currently under development. 
It aims to send Indian astronauts, known as Gaganauts, to low Earth orbit. The programme 
involves extensive crew training, development of a human-rated launch vehicle, and an 
advanced crew module with life support systems.

ISRO has also demonstrated capabilities in reusable launch vehicles and scramjet engine 
technology, positioning India as a growing force in the global commercial space market 
through its commercial arm, NewSpace India Limited (NSIL).

The agency's guiding philosophy has always been to use space technology for the benefit 
of common people, with a focus on practical applications in agriculture, disaster relief, 
education, and connectivity — particularly for India's vast rural population.
